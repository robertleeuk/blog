The Geography of Bliss
Eric Weiner

- But nowadays, not only do people think they can obtain happiness, but they expect it as a matter of course. Therefore, many of us, including myself, suffer from a unique modern disease. It is the illness that historian Darrin McMahon called "the unhappiness of not being happy."
- Happiness is not within us but out there. Or more precisely, the line dividing the outside from the inside is not as clear as we think.
- He explains. That's not it, he says. The Swiss are happy because they go to great lengths not to arouse envy in others. The Swiss instinctively know that envy is a great enemy of happiness, so they do whatever it takes to stamp it out. Dieter takes a sip of beer and says, "Our way of thinking is not to shine too bright a light on yourself. Otherwise, you might get shot."
- In Switzerland, only 28 percent of respondents said they preferred stores that sold a variety of ice cream flavors. Freedom of choice leads to happiness only when choosing important matters. Voting is important. Ice cream is important. But choosing one flavor out of fifty is not.
- We have far more words to describe unpleasant states than pleasant ones. When unhappy, we can choose from a buffet of expressions: depressed, melancholy, miserable, disgruntled, gloomy, discouraged, downhearted, dispirited, dejected. But when happy, the buffet shrinks to a Pizza Hut salad bar. Elated, satisfied, joyful. But these expressions cannot capture the subtle intensity of happiness.

Three Body Problem, Part 1
Liu Cixin

- Standing beneath the glittering sky, he suddenly felt the universe was small. So small that he seemed trapped alone within it. The universe was a heart or a womb, and the pervasive red light was the translucent blood filling it, and he floated suspended in that blood. The irregular flashing of the red light was like the beating of a heart or womb. There, he felt a strange and vast existence that human wisdom could never understand.
- I am a pacifist of this world. It was fortunate that I received your information first. I warn you. Do not answer! Do not answer! Do not answer!
- Come here. I will help you obtain this world. Our civilization has already lost the ability to solve its own problems. We need your strength.
- Human civilization was nurturing a powerful force of division within itself.
- All emotions—fear, sorrow, happiness, beauty—were things the Trisolaran civilization had to avoid and eliminate with all its might. Emotions made individuals and society mentally vulnerable, putting them at a disadvantage in surviving in this world's harsh environment. What the Trisolaran world needed was coldness and apathy. Over two hundred cycles of civilization history had proven that civilizations that took these two qualities as their foundation had the strongest survival ability.
- Behold, this is the insect. The difference between insect technology and ours is far greater than the difference between us and Trisolaran civilization. Humanity has mobilized every method to exterminate them. Spraying pesticides from airplanes, raising natural predators, destroying eggs, using genetic modification to eliminate reproduction. Burning them, drowning them, stocking pesticides in every home and equipping office desks with fly swatters as weapons against them. This long war has always accompanied human civilization, and victory is still undecided. The insects have not gone extinct; they still roam everywhere as before. Their numbers have not decreased since humans appeared. The Trisolarans, who see humanity as insects, seem unaware that insects have never been conquered.
